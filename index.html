Current work

  • 2026 –

    Real-time audio declipping

    Clipping flattens the peaks of an audio signal, and declipping estimates the lost peaks from the samples that survived. My published declipping methods work in batch, on the whole recording at once. This project builds streaming versions of them and measures what low latency costs in quality, comparing them against the original batch algorithms and a small learned causal model. The first release has a clipping simulator, the metrics, a least-squares baseline and the benchmark harness; latency and quality numbers will be reported as each milestone is measured.

  • 2026

    Tracking an unknown number of aircraft with a miscalibrated radar

    Two radars watch inbound aircraft, and one of them has a range offset and alignment error that nobody has measured. A sequential reversible-jump MCMC tracker samples which aircraft exist, where they are and what the radar's bias is, all at once, then projects each track forward with its uncertainty. On 20 synthetic scenarios it removes the duplicate tracks a standard GNN-EKF tracker builds when the bias is ignored, cutting the mean OSPA error from 453 m to 86 m, and it brings the position NEES down from 12.5 to 2.2, close to the ideal of 2. It adapts the RJ-MCMC and blind distortion identification from my audio-restoration research to multi-target tracking, and the write-up includes where its uncertainty is still too small.

  • 2026 –

    ASR robustness and intelligibility-aware speech enhancement

    How much does recognition accuracy fall as input quality drops, and for which systems? I compare a commercial recognizer with open-source models such as Whisper across controlled degradations, then use learned quality and intelligibility predictors as training objectives for enhancement, so the enhancer optimizes for what the recognizer and the listener need rather than for signal-level scores.
